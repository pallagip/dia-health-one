Research method · follow-up note · 20 November 2026

From dimensions to testable bets.

An earlier note argued for comparing opposing design hypotheses. This follow-up is about the step after that: how a design map becomes a set of comparisons someone can actually run, and which counts must stay apart while you do it.

Scope: this note reports no new research result and does not change program status. It describes bookkeeping for future comparisons. No molecule is proposed, enumerated, simulated, screened or ranked here.
Keep the levels apart

A map is not one long list.

The most common error in a design map is to flatten everything into a single count. Questions, variables, structures, contexts and readouts behave differently, and mixing them makes a plan look larger than it is.

A strategic question is something a team argues about: does ring rigidity matter more than linker length? An operational variable is a setting someone can choose: how long the linker is, where the attachment site sits. A unique structure is one specific, sanitized molecule with a defined synthetic route. A context or method is the buffer, the glucose level, the assay or the simulation protocol a structure is tested in. A measured readout is what the test reports back.

Readouts are the level people most often misfile. Response sharpness and conformational-change size are outcomes. Receptor accessibility can be a design objective, but its measured value is a readout for a defined structure and context. You choose a structure and context, then measure the outcome. Counting the design objective and the measurement as separate inputs can count the same effect twice.

The 19 November note listed insulin-receptor accessibility as a design dimension. This follow-up separates that design objective from its readout: accessibility is measured for a defined structure in a specified context.

What the sketch numbers mean

Proposed bookkeeping, nothing more.

The planning draft discusses growing roughly 20 strategic dimensions toward about 30 axes, then about 100 operational variables and on the order of 5,000 comparison bets across variables, conditions and methods. These are planning and scoping figures only.

About 30 axes

A human-sized agenda.

A count of topics a team could debate and prioritize. It is a proposal for how wide the agenda should be, not an approved program decision and not a list of agreed design choices.

About 100 variables

Settings, once an axis is specific.

A proposal for how finely each axis might be broken into choosable settings. The public research record does not establish a reviewed or approved variable set; naming a variable does not authorize testing it.

About 5,000 comparison bets

Across variables, conditions and methods.

This is a planning estimate across design variables, test conditions and comparison methods. Only design-variable rungs can introduce new structures; condition and method comparisons reuse structures. It is not an enumerated or executed set.

These figures are presented in the planning draft as bookkeeping and scoping proposals. They do not establish a validated schema, an enumerated library, a simulation or a result. The public research record remains the place for verified outputs; treating these counts as a delivered platform would overstate the work.

Counting designs honestly

A large number is not a library.

Our earlier note described a theoretical combinatorial design space. That description is not a count of structures we enumerated, validated or tested.

The narrow theoretical claim is only that combining the listed choices without constraint yields a very large arithmetic space.

So count the levels separately and say which one you mean. Unique, sanitized, route-defined structures are one count. Contexts are another. Methods, conformers and protonation states are another. Results are another again. Multiplying them together produces a headline number that answers no question anyone asked, and it hides the only count that matters at the start: how many distinct molecules a team could realistically make and test.

Design the comparison

Opposite bets need meaningful settings.

An opposite bet is only as good as the two settings it compares. Both sides must be chemically and biologically sensible, and both must be appropriate to the assay or calculation that will judge them.

Start with the broadest contrast that still makes scientific sense in the chosen assay — two settings far enough apart to separate a real effect from noise, and both inside the range the assay can report. Extremes that no assay tolerates are not a comparison. A pH 1 to pH 14 ladder is an illustration, not a proposed experiment. Those extremes would not test the intended protein behavior under usable assay conditions.

Refine only on two conditions. First, the measured or modelled difference must exceed its own uncertainty. Second, the next, narrower comparison must be able to change a decision. If either condition fails, stop and record the interval reached. A broad contrast is useful only if it can change the next decision.

Not every choice is a ladder. An attachment site, a backbone, a sensor chemotype: these are categorical. They are compared as matched pairs under identical conditions, with no middle point to step toward. Dressing a categorical choice up as a numeric staircase invents precision the question does not have.

Models follow labels

Architecture is set by data, not by ambition.

A map of comparisons is not a training set. What a model can be is decided by the labels that exist and by how it performs on validated benchmarks — not by how many entries a planning document lists.

  • A long axis list is not a feature vector. Features that are mostly absent or imputed teach a model the imputation, not the chemistry.
  • Any model needs an honest held-out evaluation against reference cases before its outputs are used for anything.
  • Predictions without calibrated uncertainty cannot support a decision, and uncertainty itself has to be checked against measured data.

Nothing of this kind is reported here. The planning draft's model ideas are proposals, not implemented tools. This note reports no high-dimensional feature model, graph neural network, trained model, candidate ranking or production campaign, and authorizes none.

Keep the record complete

Counts and uncertainty stay visible.

Bookkeeping earns its place only if it records what happened to every comparison, including the ones that failed to separate.

Say which count you mean

Report questions, variables, unique structures, contexts and results as separate numbers with stated definitions. Do not combine them into a single headline figure.

Preserve stopped bets

Keep comparisons that ended at the broad contrast, with the same inputs, method details and reasoning as the ones that continued. A negative comparison is a result.

Record the interval, not the winner

When refinement stops, record where the difference fell inside its uncertainty. That interval is the finding; naming a winner beyond it overstates what was measured.

Evidence boundary

Bookkeeping is not evidence.

This note reports no new research result and does not change the public program status. The figures discussed here come from a planning draft and are proposals, not approved program decisions, completed files or a validated platform.

No Dia Health One compound is a measured hit, a working switch, an insulin candidate or a drug. Candidate ranking is not authorized, and no set of candidates is ready for a laboratory. The public status page remains the bounded record of what has been shown.
