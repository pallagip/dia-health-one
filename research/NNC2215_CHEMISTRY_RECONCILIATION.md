# NNC2215 chemistry reconciliation

**Updated:** 26 October 2026
**Decision:** Source-derived B1 glucoside and B29 linker–roof product components match the element-labeled adjacency in both corrected, model-built coordinate candidates. The B1 component has one exact mapping per state and matches the beta-D-glucose reference graph; its B1–B22 fragment mass rounds to the Nature SI value. The neutral B29 graph has 16 mappings per state and eight bond-label transfer classes. A separate, explicitly conditional pH 7.4 tri-carboxylate audit treats each carboxylate C–O pair as resonance-equivalent, reducing those eight bond-label classes to one chemistry-equivalent class per state. It does not choose oxygen names or a unique coordinate mapping. Neither audit validates the complete insulin conjugate, a measured protonation microstate, full-system charges, force-field parameters, or a production model.

## Evidence reviewed

The 2024 synthesis route says the B29 Lys ε-amino group is first modified with O-succinimidyl pentyn-1-oxycarbonyl at pH 10.5. A B1 O-peracetyl-D-glucoside active ester is coupled near pH 7.5 and then deacetylated. The B29 terminal alkyne is joined to the macrocycle roof propyl azide by copper-catalysed azide–alkyne cycloaddition. The resulting product is reported as C325H461N79O97S6. The article identifies the B29 Lys attachment and B1 Phe N-terminal glycoside attachment; the patent's Example 2 describes the same conjugate and reports the matching calculated mass-to-charge values. See the [2024 article](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-08042-3), its [Supporting Information](https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs41586-024-08042-3/MediaObjects/41586_2024_8042_MOESM1_ESM.pdf), and [WO2023144240A1 Example 2](https://patents.google.com/patent/WO2023144240A1/en).

The coordinate inputs are the author-supplied, model-built open and closed structures by Eva Johansson and Daniele Granata, [“3D models of NNC2215”](https://springernature.figshare.com/articles/dataset/3D_models_of_NNC2215/26526460), Figshare DOI 10.6084/m9.figshare.26526460, licensed CC BY 4.0. The PDBs linked in this report are modified coordinate candidates derived from those files; they are not the original author files or experimentally solved structures.

The numbered schemes identify six B29 linker carbons (source C3–C8) and the three-carbon roof propyl-azide tail (source C21–C23). Formula arithmetic in [the existing audit](data/nnc2215-model-formula-audit.json) gives a desB30 insulin parent of C253H376N64O75S6 and an attachment difference of C72H85N15O22. The B1 attachment accounts for C10O8 heavy atoms. The B29 linker–roof appendage, excluding the LysB29 NZ endpoint, consists of the reported C56H63N15O12 macrocycle roof propyl azide plus the C6H6O2 B29 linker after the NHS leaving group departs, giving C62N15O14 heavy atoms.

The publisher's model PDBs have a 113-node/120-edge heavy-atom adjacency match for the selected PHX/LYX/MC and attachment scope, after documented open/closed LYX name aliases. [The connectivity audit](data/nnc2215-connectivity-audit.json) confirms that these records give adjacency only; PDB CONECT/LINK data do not encode bond order or formal charge.

The isolated roof propyl-azide structure from Extended Data Fig. 8i is recorded as an atom-mapped SMILES in [`structures/nnc2215-roof-propyl-azide.smi`](structures/nnc2215-roof-propyl-azide.smi), with a graph-ID legend in [`data/nnc2215-roof-macrocycle-atom-map.json`](data/nnc2215-roof-macrocycle-atom-map.json). The numbered roof tail C21–C23 is explicitly associated with map IDs 78–80; other IDs are stable graph identifiers, not authoritative PDB atom names.

This graph uses the neutral COOH form shown in the source drawing and one conventional C–N=N+=N− azide resonance form. It is an isolated component graph, not the complete insulin conjugate. PDB correspondence, protonation at simulation pH, charges, force-field parameters, and production-MD readiness remain unvalidated. The source drawing's positional labels around C16/C17 should be checked against the high-resolution figure before using those labels as coordinate atom names.

The mapped 83-heavy-atom roof graph embeds in both corrected open and closed coordinate candidates under the six previously documented C21–C23/azide-tail anchors. Each state has 16 element-matched adjacency embeddings; 54 of the 83 graph map IDs have more than one candidate PDB atom across those embeddings. This does not produce a unique full graph-to-PDB atom map. The state-by-state mappings, ambiguity counts, anchor provenance, candidate PDB hashes, and unmatched atoms are in [`data/nnc2215-roof-graph-coordinate-mapping-audit.json`](data/nnc2215-roof-graph-coordinate-mapping-audit.json); regenerate it with [`audit_nnc2215_roof_graph_coordinate_mapping.py`](audit_nnc2215_roof_graph_coordinate_mapping.py).

Each selected LYX B30 + MC C1 fragment contains 91 heavy atoms (C62N15O14). Eight unmatched LYX atoms—CD1, CD2, C1, C2, CD3, CD4, O/OD, and O1—are attributed to the B29 linker under the existing candidate partition. The matching uses element labels and CONECT/LINK adjacency only; it does not check bond orders, use a geometric fit, validate stereochemistry, or independently prove the six anchors or the linker interpretation. The audit confirms the corrected PDB input hashes and does not modify either coordinate file.

## Two-carbon discrepancy

Both source model states contain 64 carbon, 15 nitrogen and 14 oxygen records in the LYX/MC B29-linked pool, against 62 carbon, 15 nitrogen and 14 oxygen expected from the synthesis-derived formula. The two-carbon surplus is present in both open and closed models.

The [candidate linker map](data/nnc2215-linker-mapping-audit.json) follows the six-carbon B29 linker and the three-carbon roof tail through serial-level PDB connectivity. Under that candidate partition, two tail carbons fall in LYX and one falls in MC, leaving 54 source macrocycle carbons expected in MC. The PDB MC residue has 56 carbon records.

The [coordinate-reconciliation candidate](data/nnc2215-carbon-reconciliation-candidate.json) removes MC C27/C29 and their four hydrogens, then connects the retained methyl carbons C28/C30 directly to aryl carbons C73/C75. This matches the published direct aryl methyl substituents and the SI's six-proton H-18 assignment. An independent enumeration of pairwise contractions among 18 degree-two carbon vertices found that C27/C29 is the only pair that yields the source-derived graph, in both states, within that edit class. The corrected B29-linked pool becomes C62N15O14 in both states, and the full heavy-atom inventory becomes C325N79O97S6. This is a source-supported coordinate-repair inference, not an author-provided atom map.

The candidate PDBs reopen successfully with all connectivity references resolved, no duplicate atom names within residues, strictly increasing atom serials, and records no longer than 80 columns. Both new aryl-methyl bonds are 1.50 Å and their C-H bonds are 1.09 Å; the local methyl-hydrogen overlap screen passes. In the open model, ASN A21's misplaced HXT record was removed and an OXT was reconstructed in the CA-C-O plane (C-OXT 1.25 Å; O-C-OXT 119.66°). These checks validate file consistency and local geometry only. They do not validate the full graph, stereochemistry, protonation, or force-field energy.

The synthesis schemes and patent establish the intended product, linker chemistry, reaction order and product composition. The source-derived B29 linker–roof component has an exact element/adjacency match to each corrected candidate, but the scaffold mapping and neutral bond-label transfer remain ambiguous. The candidate methyl correction remains a source-supported inference, not an authoritative atom map. The original publisher PDBs still have a two-carbon residual and the open model lacks ASN A21 OXT; these are properties of those source files, not of the corrected candidates.

### Declared pH 7.4 tri-carboxylate scenario — 26 October 2026

The audit models the three macrocycle acids as deprotonated for a conditional pH 7.4 graph comparison. It removes three protons from the neutral B29 component (C62H72N16O14 to C62H69N16O14; local charge change −3) and represents each carboxylate with two equivalent C–O edges sharing a resonance label and numeric bond order 1.5. This is a graph-equivalence convention, not a literal localized bond assignment, pKa calculation, measured microstate, partial-charge assignment, or complete NNC2215 charge ledger. No PDB oxygen labels are selected, coordinates are unchanged, and the remainder of insulin is outside this component audit. The full-system protonation inventory and total charge remain open.

The [Nature Fig. 2a product drawing](https://media.springernature.com/full/springer-static/image/art%3A10.1038%2Fs41586-024-08042-3/MediaObjects/41586_2024_8042_Fig2_HTML.png) places the roof substituent at triazole N1 and the B29 linker at C4, supporting the source-derived 1,4-disubstituted 1,2,3-triazole assignment. This assignment comes from the product drawing, not PDB adjacency or distances. The PDB CONECT/LINK records encode adjacency only; they do not establish bond order, formal charge, or stereochemistry.

## B1 beta-D-glucoside graph and candidate audit — 25 October 2026

The mapped B1 component is recorded in [`structures/nnc2215-b1-glucoside-attachment.smi`](structures/nnc2215-b1-glucoside-attachment.smi), with source roles and graph evidence in [`data/nnc2215-b1-glucoside-atom-map.json`](data/nnc2215-b1-glucoside-atom-map.json). It contains 19 mapped atoms including the PheB1 N boundary placeholder and 19 edges. Removing the linker while retaining and protonating the glycosidic oxygen yields C6H12O6 and matches the beta-D-glucose PubChem reference InChIKey WQZGKKKJIJFFOK-VFUOTHLCSA-N. Accurate RDKit CIP labels by source glucose carbons C1–C5 are R/R/S/S/R. This comparison caught and corrected three inverted SMILES tags at glucose C2, C3, and C5.

The B1 audit in [`data/nnc2215-b1-topology-audit.json`](data/nnc2215-b1-topology-audit.json) finds one exact element-labeled adjacency mapping for each of the open and closed candidates. From the B1–B22 sequence, reduced cysteines, free peptide termini, and net B1 attachment increment C10H16O8, it derives C120H187N31O39S2 and [M+2H]2+ 1376.15947, which rounds to the SI's calculated 1376.2. The reported measured value is 1376.6. These are graph and fragment-composition checks; the model PDBs do not establish bond orders, formal charge, protonation, or stereochemistry. The N–H record also differs between candidates: the open PheB1 N has an explicit CONECT hydrogen, while the closed model has no CONECT edge to its nearby PHX H2A record (1.006 Å away). Re-run with [`audit_nnc2215_b1_topology.py`](audit_nnc2215_b1_topology.py) using `micromamba run -p /Users/andrii/scratch/dia-target-tools/env python research/audit_nnc2215_b1_topology.py`.

For each state, the audit records 1,584 mapped edge-distance measurements (99 edges across 16 mappings). Distances span 1.2116–1.5611 Å in the open candidate and 1.2116–1.5548 Å in the closed candidate. Fourteen of 16 mappings per state have one to three heuristic short carboxyl C–O distances outside broad plausibility intervals; two mappings per state are fully within the screen. The intervals use covalent-radius sums with disclosed heuristic bond-class offsets, based on Cordero et al., “Covalent radii revisited” (2008), [DOI 10.1039/B801115J](https://doi.org/10.1039/B801115J). These distances neither establish bond order nor select a mapping.

Hydrogen-neighbor records differ at roof C22/C23 and linker C4–C6. At map 85 (linker C4), the open candidate records HC1 and HXTA while the closed candidate records HXTA only, one fewer hydrogen record. At roof maps 79/80 and linker maps 86/87, the recorded hydrogen names differ while counts match. These are CONECT-record observations, not validated hydrogen chemistry.

The inputs are corrected candidate PDB derivatives of publisher-supplied model-built coordinates, not experimentally determined structures. The selected graph is a source-derived component, not a complete insulin topology. The audit validates no simulation-pH protonation, formal charges, stereochemistry, force-field parameters, target-binding MD, or clinical conclusion; it is not a drug or production simulation model. The audit used CPU only.

## Provisional chemical assignments

These are source-informed expectations for a future graph review, not final force-field assignments:

- The B29 Lys ε-amino group is converted to a carbamate in the pentynyl linker. The source product drawing supports the 1,4-disubstituted 1,2,3-triazole; the candidate PDBs do not independently establish that bond labeling.
- The B1 Phe α-amino group is acylated by the glucoside linker and is neutral in that conjugated form. The deprotected β-D-glucoside graph and mapped C1–C5 stereochemistry are now verified against the source scheme, the SI anomeric coupling, and a beta-D-glucose reference structure. PDB adjacency does not establish stereochemistry.
- The macrocycle has three reported carboxylic-acid groups. This audit models all three as carboxylates in a declared pH 7.4 scenario; that is an assumption, not a measured dominant microstate or a pKa result. The complete charge assignment still depends on the resolved graph and pKa/microstate assumptions.
- The existing isolated PaG audit supports the singly deprotonated glucuronate, net charge −1, and five CIP labels S, S, R, S, S. It validates the isolated ligand files only; it does not validate the NNC2215 conjugate.
- The full insulin system requires an explicit pH 7.4 protonation inventory, including terminal groups, ionizable side chains, disulfides, and the conjugation sites. A pH label alone does not define that inventory.

The current PDB records also disagree around hydrogen ownership: the closed model assigns a nearby PheB1 N hydrogen to PHX without a CONECT edge, and the LYX CD2 hydrogen-neighbour count differs between states. These are additional unresolved topology records.

## Evidence required to close chemistry

1. Join the source-mapped B1 and B29 attachment components to the insulin backbone and modification sites in a complete atom-mapped conjugate graph; independently check the complete graph against the synthesis schemes and patent.
2. Independently review the candidate MC C27/C29 removal and C28/C30 aryl-methyl mapping, refine the ambiguous B29 graph-to-coordinate mappings, and reconcile the differing hydrogen/link records. The B1 component already has a one-to-one adjacency mapping in each state.
3. Explicit bond orders, aromaticity, remaining complete-conjugate stereochemistry, protonation microstates, formal-charge ledger and integer total charge for each simulated condition. The source drawing supports the 1,4 CuAAC regiochemistry, while the candidate coordinates do not resolve scaffold bond-label transfer.
4. A versioned structure file with the validated graph, atom names and source links. Compare the mapped source graph against the reported formula and HRMS.
5. Only after items 1–4 pass: select one force-field family and validate custom macrocycle/linker parameters, carbohydrate compatibility, PaG, torsions/conformers, charge sums and prepared-system energies.
